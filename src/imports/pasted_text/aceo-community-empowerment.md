## Ajokon Goat Empowerment Project

### Building Financial Security Through Productive Assets

The Ajokon Goat Empowerment Project (AGEP) builds on the traditional Ajokon livestock-sharing practice and strengthens it with structured agreements, training, monitoring, accountability, and community-based support mechanisms.

Selected households receive goats as productive assets. As the animals reproduce, beneficiaries retain offspring while the parent goats are transferred to another participating household. This creates a cycle of asset sharing that allows the benefits of the program to reach more families over time.

# The Ajokon Plus Model

## Traditional Wisdom Strengthened for Sustainable Development

The Ajokon Plus Model is an enhanced version of the traditional Ajokon practice. It combines indigenous community-based livestock sharing with clear structures for accountability, monitoring, savings, and long-term sustainability.

The model is designed around one central principle:

**A productive asset should not benefit only one household. It should create an opportunity for other households to become economically secure as well.**

## How the Model Works

### 1. Households Receive Productive Assets

Selected households receive a pair of goats under a formal community agreement and participate in training on livestock care, asset management, and program responsibilities.

### 2. Families Care for and Manage the Goats

Beneficiary households take responsibility for proper feeding, housing, health monitoring, breeding, and general livestock management.

### 3. Community Monitoring

Community monitors conduct routine and, where necessary, random follow-up visits to assess animal welfare, household participation, and compliance with the program agreement.

### 4. Offspring Strengthen the Household

When the goats reproduce, the household retains the agreed offspring, creating a growing productive asset base and a potential source of future income.

### 5. Parent Goats Are Rotated

The parent goats are transferred to another eligible beneficiary household, allowing the empowerment cycle to continue.

### 6. The Community Goat Bank Grows

Additional offspring may contribute to the Community Goat Bank, creating a communal reserve that can support future beneficiaries and help replace animals when necessary.

### 7. Families Build an Emergency Safety Net

Beneficiaries participate in the Ajokon Community Savings Circle, helping households prepare for emergencies without being forced to sell productive assets during times of financial stress.

# Ajokon Community Savings Circle (ACSC)

## A Community-Based Emergency Safety Net

Unexpected expenses can quickly undermine household livelihoods. Illness, school costs, and other emergencies can force families to sell productive assets at the worst possible time.

The Ajokon Community Savings Circle (ACSC) is designed to help prevent this.

Each participating household contributes an agreed amount to a community-managed savings fund. When an urgent need arises, eligible members can access short-term, recoverable financial support.

The savings mechanism helps households:

* Respond to emergencies without selling livestock
* Protect productive household assets
* Develop a culture of saving
* Strengthen financial discipline
* Build trust and collective responsibility
* Increase household resilience

### The Asset-Sharing Cycle

**1 — Receive**
Beneficiary families receive two goats together with training in livestock care and asset management.

**2 — Grow**
Families care for the goats and retain agreed offspring after reproduction.

**3 — Share**
Parent goats are transferred to another beneficiary family, allowing the empowerment cycle to continue.

**4 — Protect**
The Community Goat Bank and savings mechanism provide additional support for asset protection and continuity.

# How We Implement AGEP

AGEP follows a structured implementation process designed to promote transparency, community ownership, animal welfare, and long-term sustainability.

## 1. Identification of Beneficiaries (image)

Potential beneficiaries are identified and assessed according to established eligibility criteria. The process seeks to prioritize households that can responsibly manage the livestock and actively participate in the program.

## 2. Formation of the Beneficiary Leadership Committee (image)

Participating beneficiaries establish a leadership committee responsible for supporting coordination, communication, accountability, and cooperation among members.

## 3. Procurement, Treatment, and Identification of Goats(image)

Goats are procured and undergo appropriate health checks and treatment before distribution. Animals are identified and tagged to support monitoring and accountability.

## 4. Beneficiary Training(image)

Beneficiaries are brought together for practical training covering livestock management, animal health, disease prevention, feeding, housing, breeding, record keeping, and basic veterinary practices.

## 5. Distribution of Goats(image)

Goats are distributed to approved beneficiary households according to the program's distribution plan and formal participation agreements.

## 6. Community Leadership and Cooperation(image)

The chairperson and beneficiary leadership committee encourage cooperation among participating households and help ensure that members understand and fulfill their responsibilities.

## 7. Official Project Commencement(image)

Following distribution and completion of the required preparations, the project officially commences. Monitoring, community support, asset rotation, savings activities, and capacity-building continue throughout implementation.
